Suggested path

Both documents are written for a technical reader who has already run the NetBird open-source build and wants to know what production looks like.

01

Start with the presentation

It sets the shape of the migration in ten minutes: where the overlay sits, what changes at each phase, and what the network looks like when it is done.

02

Then the write-up, in order or by section

Each phase answers its questions under its own heading, so it can be read straight through or jumped into. Assumptions and open items sit at the end of each phase.

03

Bring the open items to the walkthrough

A handful of decisions need Summit's input, particularly around identity hosting and how the assessor scopes CUI-bearing paths. Those are flagged rather than assumed.

If you only have five minutes: slides 3, 5 and 11 of the presentation, which are the current state, Phase 1 and the end state, then section 6 of the write-up, which is the three design decisions and their reasoning.

Scope of this design

What Part I covers, and what deliberately comes later.

This is the design across all three phases: what gets built, where it sits, how access is granted, and the order it rolls out in. It is written to be decided on, so recommendations are made rather than deferred, and where something falls outside what NetBird solves, it says so plainly.

Comes after the working session
  • Implementation runbooks and the exact FortiGate rule set, once we have walked the current policy together
  • The identity build, which depends on the provider decision in section 6 of the write-up
  • Pilot scheduling and the user list, around any change freeze on your side
  • Phase 3 site sequencing, which follows the per-site link and failover review
